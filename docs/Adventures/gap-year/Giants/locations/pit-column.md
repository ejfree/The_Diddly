## The Pit Column — The Long Throat

**Location:** The base of Zanaya's Well, where the Pit's worked stone ends and Khyber begins
**Connects:** The surface of Arenal (above) to the Outer Khyber tunnel network (below, feeding into [pit-to-g1-connector.md](pit-to-g1-connector.md), Zone 1 — Pit Outflow)
**Structure:** A single vast vertical shaft, roughly 300 feet in diameter, with a natural stone column rising through its center. A giant-cut ramp spirals around the column from top to bottom.
**Height:** Approximately 900 feet from the hidden surface opening to the camp floor at the bottom.
**Inhabitants:** A permanent hill giant garrison (Gullet Camp), rotating patrols, pack animals, and whatever the giants have dragged down with them.

---

### Overview

This is what "the bottom of the Pit does not end — it opens" actually means.

Where Zanaya's Well should terminate, the worked stone gives way all at once to raw open air. The floor simply stops. Beyond the last dressed archway of the Well, the party stands on a ledge overlooking a shaft so wide that a thrown torch tumbles for a full ten seconds before the light disappears. This is **the Long Throat** — a natural chimney in the rock, ancient beyond dwarven record, connecting the wild high country of Arenal's surface directly down into Outer Khyber.

It should be impassable. It isn't, because something spent years making it otherwise.

Rising from the shaft's floor, a colossal natural column of stone — thick as a keep, easily 80 feet across — climbs the full height of the shaft like a spine, nearly but not quite touching the unseen ceiling far above. Wrapped around this column, cut and packed and reinforced by giant hands, is a ramp: wide enough for three hill giants to walk abreast, spiraling downward in long switchback loops from a crude gate at the surface all the way to the shaft floor. It is not elegant. It is not even especially safe. It is exactly wide enough and exactly strong enough to move giants, livestock, and plunder between the upper world and the Steading's road network, and not one foot wider than that.

This is how the hill giants of the Steading reach the surface to raid, and how they haul what they steal back down into Khyber. Zanaya's Well and the connector tunnels are the quiet, hidden route south to the Steading itself. The Long Throat is the loud one — the one with traffic, torches, guards, and a permanent camp squatting at its foot.

The party's arrival at the base of Zanaya's Well places them on a narrow ledge roughly two-thirds of the way *down* the shaft, on the opposite side of the column from the ramp itself — a natural crack in the shaft wall that the giants have never bothered to seal, because nothing they know of uses it. From here, the party can observe the ramp, the column, and the camp below before deciding how to proceed.

### The Column and the Ramp

**The Column:** A natural stone pillar, octagonal in cross-section where sheared by old fault lines, roughly 80 feet in diameter and running the full height of the shaft. Its surface is scarred with centuries of giant tool-work near the ramp, but the far side (facing the party's arrival ledge) is untouched raw rock — climbable with effort (DC 20 Athletics) but offering no shortcuts; a fall from any significant height on the column's bare face means a very long drop.

**The Ramp (the giants call it "the Coil"):** Cut and reinforced stone, 25 feet wide, sloping gently enough for giants to march or drag sledges without difficulty (roughly a 1-in-8 grade). It completes eleven full loops around the column between the surface gate and the camp floor. At the outer edge, a low lip of stacked stone (2 feet high) is the only guard against the open shaft — enough to stop a dropped barrel, not remotely enough to stop a falling person.

- **Surface gate:** A crude palisade of lashed logs and packed earth conceals the ramp's top entrance amid scrub forest in the Arenal high country. It is manned in shifts (see Ramp Watch, below) and disguised well enough that DC 22 Perception is needed to spot it from outside a hundred feet.
- **Switchback landings:** Every second loop (six landings total between surface and floor) widens into a flat shelf roughly 40 feet across, used for guard posts, rope-hoist stations, and rest stops for driven livestock. Each landing has a brazier, usually lit.
- **Rope hoists:** At three landings, the giants have rigged massive rope-and-pulley systems (giant-forged chain, timber winches) to lower heavy loads — livestock, prisoners, sacks of loot, barrels of salted meat — directly down the shaft's open center rather than walking them the whole ramp. A hoist descent takes minutes instead of the hour-plus a full ramp walk requires, but it is loud, requires at least two giants to operate, and passes directly through the open shaft air where anything watching from the column's far side (the party's ledge) has a clear view and a clear shot.
- **Structural note:** The ramp is old in its bones — the giants inherited more than they built. Deep grooves and worn stone predate the Steading's occupation by a wide margin, and one switchback landing (the fourth from the top) has been reinforced over an older collapse with mismatched, non-giant masonry. DC 20 Society or Lore (Dwarven) identifies the repair work as roughly contemporary with the Sundering Range Survey (see [pit-to-g1-connector.md](pit-to-g1-connector.md), Waypoint 5) — meaning dwarves once used this shaft too, and stopped for a reason nobody has written down.

**Descent time:** Walking the full Coil from surface to floor takes hill giants (and similarly-paced parties) roughly 2 hours at a normal pace, or 1 hour moving quickly and loudly (−10 to Stealth checks for the descent). Using a rope hoist (if the party can commandeer or rig one) takes 10–15 minutes but is not subtle.

### Gullet Camp

At the base of the Long Throat, ringed around the column's foot and spilling into the mouth of the tunnel that leads to Zone 1 (Pit Outflow), sits **Gullet Camp** — the hill giants' permanent staging ground for the surface route. It is not the Steading. It is a waystation: rougher, louder, and far less defended in depth, because nothing has ever threatened it from below before.

**Read aloud (viewing the camp from the arrival ledge, or on final approach down the ramp):**
> *The shaft floor opens into a wide stone bowl, lit by a dozen greasy bonfires. Hide tents the size of barns are staked around crude corrals holding cattle, goats, and things that used to be neither. Bones are piled in careless heaps between the tents — some old and grey, some very much not. At the center, near where the ramp finally levels out, a huge lean-to of lashed timber and stolen sailcloth serves as the overseer's hall. The tunnel mouth to the south — dark, low, and reeking faintly of wet stone — is the only way out of this bowl that doesn't mean climbing back up the way you came.*

#### Layout

- **The Corrals:** Stolen livestock (cattle, goats, occasionally horses) penned in rope-and-stake enclosures near the camp's northern edge, tended by two ogre herders at any given time.
- **The Tent Rows:** Six giant-sized hide tents house the off-duty garrison. Personal effects, looted goods awaiting sorting, and simple bedding fill each one.
- **The Overseer's Hall:** A large lean-to at the camp's center, home to **Grukka Bonecrusher** (see below). Contains a crude "map table" — a flattened boulder scratched with a rough trade-route map of the Coil, the tunnel network south, and notations in Jotun pictographs marking recent raid targets.
- **The Bone Midden:** A refuse heap east of the tents, picked over by carrion crawlers and the occasional wandering ghoul (see Zone 1 table in [pit-to-g1-connector.md](pit-to-g1-connector.md)) that have learned to scavenge here without being caught.
- **The Southern Tunnel Mouth:** The unremarkable-looking passage that becomes Zone 1 — Pit Outflow of the connector route. Two ogre sentries are posted here at all times.

#### Garrison

- **1 Overseer:** Grukka Bonecrusher (unique, see below)
- **6 Hill Giants** (Lvl 7 each) — rotating between ramp patrol and camp duty; typically 2–3 present in camp at any time, the rest on the Coil or resting in tents
- **8 Ogres** (Lvl 3 each) — herders, haulers, and sentries; typically 4–5 present in camp
- **1d4 Dire Wolves** (Lvl 3 each) — kept as watch animals, usually chained near the tunnel mouth or the overseer's hall

**Total camp population at any given time is roughly 8–12 creatures**, with the remainder rotating on ramp duty or absent on a raid (GM's discretion — an empty or half-empty camp is a good result of good timing or a distraction elsewhere).

#### Grukka Bonecrusher — Camp Overseer

A hill giant, older and heavier than most of the garrison, missing one ear and half convinced the wound was worth the story he tells about it (it wasn't; a mule kicked him). Grukka answers to the Steading's chief and takes the job of keeping the surface route running seriously — arguably more seriously than anyone else in this operation. He is not stupid, and he is not lazy. He rotates guards properly, keeps the map table updated, and has survived several attempts by rival giants to take his post.

- **Level 9 Hill Giant (Elite)** — use the standard Hill Giant statistics with the Elite adjustment (PF2e elite template: +2 to AC, saves, Perception, and attack; +1 die to damage; increased HP)
- **Personality:** Gruff, competent, contemptuous of anyone who wastes his time. Will negotiate briefly out of professional curiosity before violence — mostly to figure out how intruders got past the ramp watch, which offends his pride more than it worries him.
- **Loot:** A giant-sized signal horn (used to call for reinforcements from the Steading via runner — not instantaneous, but the alarm will eventually travel), a crude ledger of raid tallies scratched on hide, and a personal stash hidden beneath his cot: 900 gp in mixed coin, a small chest of surface jewelry (600 gp), and a *potion of fire breath* (minor) he won in a bet and has never used because he doesn't trust it.

### Getting Through: Stealth, Bluff, or Fight

The party has three broad approaches to Gullet Camp, all of which then lead to the same destination: the southern tunnel mouth into Zone 1 (Pit Outflow) of [pit-to-g1-connector.md](pit-to-g1-connector.md).

**1. The Far Ledge Route (Stealth):** From the arrival ledge, a careful party can work along the shaft wall on the column's untouched face, bypassing the ramp and the camp's northern edge entirely, and drop into the southern tunnel mouth from above without ever setting foot in the camp proper. This requires DC 22 Athletics (climbing) and DC 20 Stealth (avoiding the two tunnel-mouth sentries) but skips the garrison completely. Recommend this as the "clean" option for a cautious party.

**2. The Ramp (Bluff or Brute Force):** Walking the Coil openly, in giant-sized disguise, stolen giant gear, or simply banking on being mistaken for scouts/allies (bugbears, verbeeg agents, etc. — see the Ogre War Party waypoint in the connector doc for tone), is possible with a strong Deception check (DC 22) at each guard encounter. Failure means a fight, likely escalating fast given the garrison size.

**3. The Camp (Direct Assault):** A full frontal approach through the camp itself is a genuine set-piece fight against a partial or full garrison. This is a good "epic entrance to the giant chain" encounter if the party is built for it — treat it as a moderate-to-severe encounter budget depending on how much of the garrison is present, and let Grukka's signal horn create escalating stakes if the fight runs long.

**Consequence hook:** If the alarm is raised (Grukka's horn, or a fleeing giant reaching the Steading first), the Steading receives advance warning that "little ones with claws" are moving through Khyber — reinforcing the rumor already seeded by the Giant Returning Home waypoint encounter in the connector document. This can tighten patrols in Zone 4 (Steading Root) if the GM wants escalating tension as the party nears G1.

### Notes for the GM

- This location is the literal answer to "how do the giants get to the surface to raid," and it should feel that way — a working supply road, not a dungeon room. Giants here are bored, tired, or annoyed more often than they are alert; use that for tone contrast against the more dangerous wandering encounters deeper in the connector tunnels.
- The old dwarven masonry patch at the fourth landing is a deliberate breadcrumb, tying this location to the Sundering Range Survey thread already present in the connector document. It never needs to be resolved, but a curious party pulling on it can find a small side-mystery: why did the dwarves stop maintaining this shaft?
- Zanaya's Well (Arc 2.5) technically ends at the arrival ledge. Everything below that point — the shaft, the column, the ramp, and the camp — belongs to Arc 3 geography and should feel like a tonal shift: the campaign's first real giant-scale set piece, before the party has even reached G1 proper.
- If the party clears or bypasses Gullet Camp quietly, consider letting them loot the overseer's hall map table for a small preview of the Steading's raid patterns — useful worldbuilding texture and a soft nudge toward the Trail of Evidence beats in the connector document.
