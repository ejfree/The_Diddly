# The Little Church

> Alben's first proper venue — a small, intimate drinking lounge built directly above [distillerysanctum.md](distillerysanctum.md), which becomes its basement and back office. Alben calls it his "Sippin' Club." Regulars just call it the Little Church, half because of the holy symbol over the door and half because Alben genuinely treats every night behind the bar like a service. It later grows into the much larger [bigchurch.md](bigchurch.md).

---

## What This Place Is

The Little Church is one room doing three jobs at once: a bar, a fighting pit, and a shrine, none of them formally separated from the others. Alben built it the way he does everything — with more enthusiasm than architectural planning — and the result is a space that feels less like a business and more like an extension of his personality poured into four walls.

It's small enough that Alben can see every table from behind the bar and every fighter from the pit's edge at the same time. That's not an accident. He never wanted a venue so big he couldn't personally vouch for every drink poured and every fight fought under his roof.

---

## Dimensions & Capacity

The whole building is a single story roughly **40 feet by 30 feet** (about 1,200 square feet) sitting atop the [Distillery Sanctum](distillerysanctum.md) below. It reads as cozy rather than cramped — every inch of it was sized around Alben personally being able to see, hear, and reach the whole room at once.

**Typical guest count:** **20–30 patrons** on a normal night, with the room comfortably able to pack in up to **40** shoulder-to-shoulder for a well-known fight or a special pour from the Cabinet. Beyond that, people start spilling out the front door, which Alben treats as a good problem to have rather than one worth solving with a bigger room — see [Why It Stays Small](#why-it-stays-small-for-now) below.

## Layout

### The Lounge

Roughly **30 feet by 25 feet**, the bulk of the building's footprint. A single intimate room, low lighting, mismatched but comfortable furniture, the kind of place that feels like someone's living room more than a commercial bar — because for a long while, functionally, it was. Seats around **20–25** at tables and stools, with standing room for another **10–15** around the pit rail on a busy night. Regulars have their own tables. Newcomers get pulled in fast, usually by Solomon before they've even found a seat.

### The Bar

A modest **12-foot** bar counter along the back wall, just long enough for Alben to work alone without needing a second set of hands. Alben works the bar himself, every night the Church is open. This is where "Let's have a drink. You want the usual?" gets asked most often (see [liquor.md](../rambui/liquor.md#the-ritual-lets-have-a-drink)) — the Rambui behind this bar is drawn straight up from the Sanctum below, and the Cabinet's hundred-bottle collection lives here in reach of his hand: genuine kegs, purchased and delivered by magical courier, kept in his Spacious Pouches rather than a cellar (see [liquor.md](../rambui/liquor.md#stocking-the-cabinet-without-a-hundred-kegs)).

### The Fighting Pit

A sunken circular pit roughly **15 feet across** and sunk **3 feet** below the main floor, set at the room's center, ringed by a low wooden barrier just sturdy enough to keep spilled drinks (and the occasional stumbling patron) out of the action. Comfortably fits two combatants and their gear with room to move; ringside standing room holds a crowd of **10–15** watching shoulder-to-shoulder. This is where Alben's Gladiator archetype gets its actual use — impromptu bouts, sparring, the odd real grudge match, all of it happening a few feet from people finishing their second Rambui. Living for the Applause and Play to the Crowd (see [character.md](../character.md#archetype-gladiator)) were built for exactly this room.

### Solomon's Post

A small perch, barely **2 feet** across, tucked beside the front door. Solomon does not tend bar, obviously, but he **hosts** — greeting arrivals from this perch, riding his palanquin, deciding within seconds who gets the warm greeting and who gets the cool, appraising stare. Regulars swear the duck's read on a stranger is more reliable than a Perception check. Alben has never once overruled him.

### The Stairs Down

A narrow **4-foot-wide** stair behind the bar, barely wide enough for Alben's shoulders, leading down into the Sanctum (see [distillerysanctum.md](distillerysanctum.md)) — now the Church's basement and back office. Staff, when there eventually are any, know better than to go down without Alben. Regulars mostly don't know it's there at all.

## The Nightly Promise

Fights happen at the Little Church **constantly** — that's not a bug, it's the draw. Between the pit, the drink, and the general temperament of Alben's regulars, a night without at least one bout breaking out is the exception, not the rule. What keeps the place feeling like a rowdy good time instead of an actually dangerous one is a single, absolute promise Alben makes to everyone who steps inside:

> *"Nobody dies in my house. Nobody stays broken past sunup."*

In practice, that means two things, every single night, no exceptions:

1. **Stabilize the dying.** The instant anyone drops in the pit — or anywhere else under his roof — Alben is there with a *Stabilize* cantrip or a hands-on Medicine check before anyone even finishes shouting for him. He treats this as non-negotiable regardless of whose fault the fight was, how much he personally dislikes either combatant, or how full the bar is at the time.
2. **Heal everyone back to full by the end of the night.** Whatever damage was dealt in the pit — bruises, broken ribs, the occasional genuinely serious wound — Alben makes the rounds before closing and heals every combatant back to full using his Divine Font *Heal* slots (see [spells.md](../spells.md#divine-font--healing-font-additional-slots)). Nobody leaves the Little Church worse off than they arrived, win or lose. This is as much a part of his reputation as the Rambui is, and regulars trust the pit specifically *because* of it — a serious injury here costs a few rounds of pain and a story to tell, not a trip to a real temple.

This promise is the reason the pit gets used as freely as it does. Newcomers are occasionally shocked at how casually people throw themselves into a fight at the Little Church; regulars just shrug and point at Alben behind the bar. He's never broken the promise. He's not about to start.

## The Bunks

Above and behind the lounge, tucked into whatever space the building's growth has left over, Alben keeps a handful of simple bunks for anyone who needs a bed for the night rather than a walk home.

- **Price:** **10 gp** for a night in the bunks.
- **Included:** A cot, a roof, and **breakfast the next morning** — sourced fresh from the bakery next door.
- **The Arrangement:** The bakery owner provides breakfast for every bunk guest in exchange for **free alcohol from Alben's own stock**, a standing trade neither of them has ever bothered writing down. Alben considers it one of his better deals — he trades something he has an effectively bottomless supply of (see [inventory.md](../inventory.md) and [liquor.md](../rambui/liquor.md)) for warm bread and a good relationship with his neighbor, and everybody wins. The bakery owner, for their part, has never once complained about the arrangement, and regulars suspect they get the better end of it.
- **Who Uses Them:** Regulars who've had one Rambui too many to make it home safely, out-of-towners passing through, and — often enough — whoever just took the worse end of a fight in the pit and would rather sleep it off under Alben's roof than stumble home. Given the Nightly Promise above, anyone healed up after a bout is already on their feet again by the time the bunks would matter, but a night's rest is still on offer for anyone who wants it.

## How a Night at the Church Works

1. Alben opens the bar, Solomon takes his post.
2. Drinks flow via the standard ritual (see [liquor.md](../rambui/liquor.md#the-ritual-lets-have-a-drink)) — the usual, Omakase, or a called drink from the Flask of Fellowship.
3. If someone wants to fight — a friendly bout, a real dispute, or just Alben in the mood to burn off energy — the pit gets used, with Alben as likely to be the one fighting as refereeing. Anyone who drops gets stabilized on the spot (see The Nightly Promise above).
4. Before closing, Alben makes the rounds and heals every combatant from that night's fights back to full.
5. Late in the night, once the crowd thins, Alben slips down to the Sanctum to check a batch, top off tomorrow's stock, or just sit with Solomon and breathe — or, for anyone who's paid for a bunk, up to bed for the night.

## Why It Stays Small (For Now)

Alben likes the Little Church precisely because it's small enough to run himself. No staff he doesn't fully trust, no regulars he hasn't personally sized up, no fight in the pit he isn't within arm's reach of stopping. It is, in miniature, exactly the venue he wants to be known for — before ambition (his own, or a patron's, or simply demand outstripping what one room can hold) eventually pushes it into something much bigger. See [bigchurch.md](bigchurch.md) for what it becomes.
