# Planar Palace Expansions

## The Opening Ceremony

The casting is a **formal ceremony every day**, and it is what officially **opens the church** for the new day. Nothing is bet, bought, or fought until it's done.

- **The portal** appears on the **north wall of the public room, in the center**.
- **The fight cards** hang on **boards flanking it**, one on the **left** and one on the **right**. The cards list the day's bouts, with the arena named at the top, once it's rolled.
- **The sequence.** Alben lights the lanterns and rakes the Pit. He **rolls the d20** in front of whoever is there. He **imagines the rolled arena** and **casts the wand** (one minute), and the shimmering doorway rises in the center of the wall. The cards go up on the boards, and he pours a drink at the **altar** and puts the bucket out.
- **After the casting,** the **church is open.** The doorway stays up until the next daily preparations.
- **The designs.** Alben has **twenty arenas rehearsed**, so the roll only decides which one he imagines as he casts. See [What the Spell Gives Him](#what-the-spell-gives-him).

## The Idea

The Pit is fifteen feet across. A **wand of *Planar Palace*** gives the church a second venue every day, because the spell lets the caster **design the palace's floor plan** as they cast it. Each day's roll picks the arena, the shimmering doorway goes up, and that night's card is held there.

### What the Spell Gives Him

Facts from the spell as published ([Archives of Nethys, *Planar Palace*](https://2e.aonsrd.com/spells/221-planar-palace), Player Core p. 350; I haven't checked the full text against the book):

- **7th-rank, uncommon, arcane and occult.** Cast time **1 minute**, range **30 feet**.
- **Duration: until the next daily preparations.** So **one layout per day**. The morning's **d20 roll** picks the venue, and it stands until the next one.
- **One entrance:** a faint, shimmering, vertical rectangle that appears within range, and that the caster can make invisible.
- **Any floor plan** he imagines, as long as it fits **40 ft wide × 40 ft deep × 30 ft tall**.
- **Up to 24 servants** attend anyone inside.

Every venue below uses that same **40 × 40 × 30 ft box**, which is **more than twice the Pit's width** (a 40-foot room against a 15-foot ring) and **nearly as tall as the church's two floors together**.

### House Rules for Every Palace

- **Where the doorway goes.** It opens in the **center of the public room's north wall**, flanked by the left and right fight cards (see [The Opening Ceremony](#the-opening-ceremony)). A bout in the palace still counts toward the [Ladder](../firstchurch.md#the-shrine).
- **Betting and buffs.** Everything in [Fighting & Gambling](../firstchurch.md#fighting--gambling) still applies. The venue is announced at the **opening ceremony**, so both fighters know it before they agree, and it's part of the **tale of the tape**.
- **The Promise holds.** *Nobody dies in my house. Nobody stays broken past sunup.* The palace is part of the house, and Alben heals in there like anywhere else.
- **The door opens from inside.** Solomon's first rule. Anyone can walk out of the doorway at any time, with no hand laid on them.
- **The servants pour, and nothing else.** The 24 servants may serve **drink**. **No food is served at the church**, palace included.
- **Spectators.** The palace holds about **twenty** onlookers on a rail or ledge, and the rest watch through the doorway from the public room, which is why most venues keep a view back.
- **Audience for each.** Seat count is the GM's call.

> **GM Note — the wand isn't on his sheet.** Alben doesn't own a wand of *Planar Palace*, and it isn't on [purchase.md](../../purchase.md). A **rank-7 wand** costs well over the 3,000 gp rank-6 wands listed there, and I haven't checked its price.
>
> **GM Note — Alben can't cast it himself.** *Planar Palace* is **arcane and occult**, and Alben is **divine**. He'd have to use **Trick Magic Item** with **Arcana or Occultism**, and he's **untrained in both (+0)**. The likely DC for a level-15 item is **34**, so he can't make it on a d20 without help. Real options: a **party or hired arcane or occult caster** activates it each morning; **Alben trains** Arcana or Occultism with the unspent 13th-level skill increase (still a hard DC, with a crit-fail lockout); or the GM rules it **shared as a church amenity**. This is the same trick as the Wand of Tailwind in [dailies.js](../../assets/scripts/dailies.js), at a much harder DC.
>
> **GM Note — open questions in the spell.** I haven't verified what happens to anyone still inside when the duration ends, whether the doorway can be moved, or whether a second casting replaces the first. Read the spell before relying on any of these. Each venue below is **flavor and terrain**, and the numbers are the GM's call.

### 1. The Starstone Cathedral

**Inspiration:** the **Test of the Starstone** in the Cathedral in Absalom, which made Cayden Cailean a god after he stumbled in drunk on a dare.

A **vaulted stone nave** with a glowing **starstone** on a dais at the far end. The floor is a **ring of three concentric rings**, and a fighter who wins a ring may step up to the next. The center ring is the smallest and most dangerous. Fights are **first to the starstone**, not to a knockout.

- **The twist:** the stone **shows what a fighter most wants** when they touch it, and changes nothing else. Nobody is hurt by it.
- **For the Ladder:** the winner of the center ring goes up a rung.
- **Hook:** someone asks to **take the real Test**. Alben has to explain what it cost his god.

### 2. The Drowned Colosseum

**Inspiration:** **Azlant**, the sunken empire, and the **Plane of Water**.

A **flooded oval arena**, glass-walled on every side, sealed under a dome of air at the top. The lower two-thirds is **water**, with a ledge above it and the audience rail on top. The servants are **water-spirits** that carry drinks to the dry ledge. Fighters pick **air or water** before the bout.

- **The twist:** fighting underwater is awkward for anyone not built for it, and the **water-breathing scrolls** in Alben's pouch matter.
- **Rules:** the GM uses the underwater rules for movement, attacks, and fire. Alben's *Water Breathing* scrolls last an hour.
- **Hook:** a fighter from a **drowned city** challenges the house.

### 3. The Brass Furnace

**Inspiration:** the **Plane of Fire** and the **City of Brass**, the efreeti city.

A **brass-plated forge-hall** with a **river of molten metal** running through the middle and **narrow bridges** across it. The air is hot enough to hurt, and the walls are hung with chains and bellows. Servants are **fire-spirits** pouring tankards of glowing spirits. Fighters can't stay in long.

- **The twist:** the **heat** wears everyone down, so bouts are short and decisive.
- **Rules:** the GM sets a **heat hazard** (a Fortitude save each few rounds), and a fall into the metal is the worst outcome. Alben's *Resist Energy* scrolls matter.
- **Hook:** an **efreeti** wants to **bet on the bouts**, and has a lot of gold.

### 4. The Cloud Arena

**Inspiration:** the **Plane of Air** and the cloud giants' floating citadels.

A **chain of floating stone platforms** drifting in open sky, joined by narrow bridges, with **no walls**. The servants are **air-spirits**, and a steady wind blows from one side. Whoever falls off the edge **drops through the floor of the palace** and is caught on a net 30 feet down.

- **The twist:** the fight is about **knocking people off**. Alben's shove, **Titan Wrestler**, and the *Tailwind* wand all matter here.
- **Rules:** the GM uses a **fall** as the knockout, with the net as the safety.
- **Hook:** a **sky-sailor** asks for a rematch on a venue where they can fly.

### 5. The Shadow Court

**Inspiration:** the **Shadow Plane**, the dim echo of the Material Plane.

A **dim ballroom** of dark stone and black glass, lit only by **pale-blue lanterns**, where **everyone's shadow** moves a half-beat late. The servants are **shadow-servants** that never speak. Fighters fight in the half-dark.

- **The twist:** the **shadows** act as **decoys**. A strike at a shadow can miss, and a fighter's own shadow can get in the way.
- **Rules:** the GM treats the room as **dim light**. *Darkness*, *Faerie Fire*, and *Truesight* matter. Alben's *Truesight* and *Faerie Fire* scroll are the counters.
- **Hook:** the **shadows** keep copies of everyone who fights here, and the copies are a little off.

### 6. The Glade of the First World

**Inspiration:** the **First World**, the realm of the fey.

An **oak glade** under a **permanent twilight sky**, ringed by toadstools and hung with glowing lanterns, set around a **mossy ring** instead of a pit. The servants are **sprites** with tiny cups. **Fights are governed by bargains**: both fighters **swear an oath** before the bell, and the glade enforces it.

- **The twist:** the **oaths** matter. A fighter who breaks their word is **cursed** until they make it right, which is mostly harmless and always embarrassing.
- **Rules:** the GM tracks the oath as a **bargain**. Cold iron is **not welcome**, and anyone who brings it is asked to leave.
- **Hook:** a **fey noble** offers a bout with a **gift** as a prize, and fey gifts always come with a catch.

### 7. The Infernal Ledger

**Inspiration:** **Hell**, **Nessus**, and the devils who rule by **contract**.

A **tiered chamber of black marble and red lacquer**, built like a **courtroom**, with a **long ledger** on the judge's bench. The servants are **imps** in clerks' robes. A fighter signs the ledger before the bout, and **every term is binding**: stakes, prizes, and **debts**.

- **The twist:** **debt fighters** can fight for their **freedom**. The ledger settles it in writing, which means **no one can say they were cheated**.
- **Rules:** the GM writes the contract as a **real document**, and Alben insists on reading it **twice**.
- **Hook:** this is the venue that exposes **who holds the debts** on the street (see [Hooks](../firstchurch.md#hooks)). Alben has strong opinions about devils, and **none of them are quiet**.

### 8. The Writhing Maw

**Inspiration:** the **Abyss** and the **demon lords**, where the ground itself is hungry.

A raw red cavern of wet stone and exposed ribs, with a **floor that shifts** and sinks without warning. The servants are **quasits** that dart between guests with cups. There are no fixed walls, so the arena reshapes itself between rounds.

- **The twist:** the room moves. Sinkholes open, ledges rise, and nobody fights from the same footing twice.
- **Rules:** the GM rolls a d6 each round for what shifts (a gap opens, a ridge rises, the floor tilts). A fighter caught in a gap climbs out with a free Athletics check.
- **Hook:** a **demon cultist** challenges Alben and wants the room to win. The room doesn't care who does.

### 9. The Boneyard Court

**Inspiration:** the **Boneyard**, **Pharasma's** realm, where the dead are judged.

A grey spire hall of pale stone with **tiers of silent, translucent spectators** along the walls: the dead of the Boneyard, watching. The servants are ghostly attendants who pour for the living and set a cup out for the dead. A fighter who is knocked out sees the court's long hall for a heartbeat.

- **The twist:** the dead are an audience, and they cheer for the fighter who takes a hit well. It's strange and a little moving.
- **Rules:** the GM gives a +1 circumstance bonus to a fighter's next check after they refuse to yield, as the dead lean in.
- **Hook:** a **ghost** in the gallery recognizes someone in the bar and leaves a message in the cup it was given.

### 10. The Glowcap Caverns

**Inspiration:** the **Darklands**, the vast underground beneath Golarion.

A natural cavern under a vault of dripping stone, lit by **glowing mushrooms** in blue, green, and violet, with spore clouds hanging in the air and pools of cold water between the pillars. The servants are fungal attendants who offer cups of fermented spore-brew.

- **The twist:** the spores make everything hazy and drift into the open mouth of anyone who gasps.
- **Rules:** the GM uses a mild spore hazard (a Fortitude save when a fighter is winded, or a minor condition on a failure). Alben's *Unfettered Movement* and cleansing spells help.
- **Hook:** something **lives in the fungus**, and it has been listening to the fights.

### 11. The Axis Tribunal

**Inspiration:** **Axis**, the plane of law, and its **inevitables** and **axiomites**.

A perfect cube of **white marble** with a spotless floor, exact right angles, and a **mechanical referee** standing in the center. The servants are axiomites. Everything is measured, and nothing is random.

- **The twist:** no luck. The referee rules on every strike, and it can't be bribed, persuaded, or distracted. It's the opposite of how Alben runs his own pit, and he finds it fascinating.
- **Rules:** the GM calls everything by the book and treats the ring as fixed difficulty. A fighter who argues loses a round.
- **Hook:** the **referee** quietly objects to Alben's own rules and asks him to justify them.

### 12. The Winter Court

**Inspiration:** **Irrisen**, the land of eternal winter ruled by the **witches of Baba Yaga**.

A ballroom of **blue ice** with a frozen floor, glittering chandeliers, and a throne of frost at the far end. The servants are frost-faced courtiers who hold steaming cups and never melt. Anyone who falls on the ice keeps sliding.

- **The twist:** the floor is slick, so footing is everything. A good shove sends a fighter across the whole room.
- **Rules:** the GM treats the floor as difficult, slippery terrain. Alben's maul and *Titan Wrestler* make him dangerous here, and his heavy armor makes him hard to move.
- **Hook:** a **winter witch** asks for a bout as a favor, and the favor has a long memory.

### 13. The Alkenstar Gunworks

**Inspiration:** **Alkenstar**, the city of gunsmiths and alchemists in the Mana Wastes.

A mechanical arena of pipes, gantries, and furnaces, with **catwalks above** and a firing range below. The servants are **clockwork porters** that carry cups on trays. Fireworks, cannon, and alchemical charges go off on a timer.

- **The twist:** bangs and flashes on a schedule. A smart fighter times attacks to the explosions.
- **Rules:** the GM sets a three-round cycle of blasts, with a Reflex save for anyone in the blast zone. Fire and sonic damage are common.
- **Hook:** a **gunsmith** wants to test a new weapon and will pay well for a volunteer.

### 14. The Monsoon Hold

**Inspiration:** Alben's own homeland, the jungle islands of **Minata** and **Atas Pulu**, and the Rainfall orcs (see [background.md](../../background.md)).

A timber hold on stilts in a jungle clearing, under a roof of huge leaves with **warm rain** falling through the canopy. The floor is slick wet timber, with ropes, vines, and beams overhead. The servants are jungle orcs in woven cloaks, pouring from bamboo flasks.

- **The twist:** climbing and swimming matter, and Alben has the advantage. It's the one venue where he is at home.
- **Rules:** the GM gives Alben the Rainfall heritage's +2 circumstance bonus to Athletics checks to Climb or Swim, and the floor is slippery.
- **Hook:** it's the first time Alben has felt **homesick** in years. Someone notices.

### 15. The Dreaming Hall

**Inspiration:** the **Dimension of Dreams**, where thought shapes the world.

A hall of soft light and drifting cloth, with no fixed walls and a **ceiling of slow stars**. The servants are dream-attendants with changing faces. No one is truly hurt here: wounds fade when a fighter wakes, and a knockout is a nap.

- **The twist:** a safe bout. It's the one venue where Alben's Promise costs nothing, so he uses it for new fighters.
- **Rules:** the GM treats all damage as nonlethal and gone at the end. A fighter who loses wakes in the public room, rested.
- **Hook:** the dreams **leak into the real church**. Someone keeps seeing a fight that never happened.

### 16. The Verdant Heartwood

**Inspiration:** the **Plane of Wood**, where the world is one living forest.

The hollow heart of a **giant tree**, with living walls of bark, floors of roots, and a canopy of glowing leaves far above. The servants are dryad-like tenders with cups of sap-brewed liquor. Roots and vines move slowly.

- **The twist:** the vines grab anyone who stands still too long and drag them toward the walls.
- **Rules:** the GM uses an entangling-terrain hazard (a fighter who ends a turn standing still makes a Reflex save or is immobilized). Fire damage is very unwelcome, and anyone who uses it is asked to stop.
- **Hook:** the **tree is alive**, and it has opinions about Alben's flaming maul.

### 17. The Maelstrom Roulette

**Inspiration:** the **Maelstrom**, the plane of chaos, where nothing stays put.

A spinning, ever-changing hall where the **walls, floor, and ceiling swap places** every few rounds. The servants are protean-like attendants that change shape. The only constant is the bell.

- **The twist:** the rules change as well as the room. Each round a different effect is in play.
- **Rules:** the GM rolls on a table of seven or eight random effects each round (gravity flips, the floor tilts, everyone swaps places). Alben loves it, as a chaotic Caydenite.
- **Hook:** a fighter who **never loses** is cheating the roulette.

### 18. The Seven Terraces

**Inspiration:** **Heaven**, the lawful good plane, a mountain of seven layers.

A mountain hall with **seven terraces**, one above the other, each a little brighter than the last. A fighter climbs one terrace for each bout won. The servants are quiet angelic attendants who pour water and wine.

- **The twist:** honor matters. A fighter who cheats or fights dirty is moved down a terrace.
- **Rules:** the GM tracks a terrace counter for each fighter, with a bonus for good conduct. Alben is uncomfortable with a venue that grades virtue.
- **Hook:** a fighter who has **never lost** wants to see the top.

### 19. The Forge of the Metal Plane

**Inspiration:** the **Plane of Metal**, where the world is iron, steel, and gleaming ore.

A vast forge-hall of gears, anvils, and chains, with **magnetic plates** in the floor and showers of sparks. The servants are clockwork smiths that ring a bell every round. Anyone in metal armor feels the pull.

- **The twist:** plate armor is a liability. The floor pulls at it, and Alben, in full plate, notices.
- **Rules:** the GM gives metal-clad fighters a penalty to movement and a chance to be pulled down. Alben's maul and shield are metal too.
- **Hook:** the forge offers to **reshape a weapon** for the price of a fight.

### 20. The Accidental Hall

**Inspiration:** **Elysium**, the home plane of **Cayden Cailean** (see [planar.md](../../plans/planar.md)).

A vast timber tavern hall with long tables, a hundred stools, a fire that never burns down, and a **ring in the middle**. The servants are happy, tipsy attendants who pour freely and don't judge. It's every church Alben has ever imagined, all at once.

- **The twist:** everyone is a friend and everyone will fight. The brawl is joyful, the drink is endless, and nobody holds a grudge.
- **Rules:** the GM treats the room as pure Caydenite ground. Alben gets a boost, the Promise is automatic, and nobody leaves hurt.
- **Hook:** the hall may be a gift, a test, or a **door to the real thing**. The god is laughing, and Alben knows it.
