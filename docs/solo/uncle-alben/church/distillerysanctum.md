# The Distillery Sanctum

> Alben's first true, fixed distillation setup — the place where Rambui stopped being something he brewed out of a travel cask on the road and became something he could *make properly*, on his own ground, on his own schedule. This is his inner sanctum: half shrine, half still, entirely personal. It later becomes the basement/back office of [littlechurch.md](littlechurch.md), and later still the much larger basement distillery beneath [bigchurch.md](bigchurch.md).

---

## What This Place Is

Before the Sanctum, Alben's Rambui was a road brewer's Rambui — mash in a sealed cask, a rough still packed in with his gear, casking whatever barrel was on hand (see [rambui.md](../rambui/rambui.md)). It was good Rambui. It was not *settled* Rambui. The Sanctum is the first place Alben ever brewed from a fixed floor instead of a saddle, and the difference shows in every corner of it: nothing here is packed for travel, and everything here is exactly where it's meant to be, because for the first time in his life, it doesn't have to be anywhere else tomorrow.

It's small. It's cluttered. It smells permanently of scorched sugar, wet stone, and rambutan pulp. It is, without question, the most sacred room Alben owns — not because anyone told him to treat it that way, but because it's the first room that was ever entirely his.

## Layout

A single low-ceilinged cellar room, stone-walled, lit by a few hanging lanterns and whatever daylight makes it down the stairs. Everything in it serves either the brew or the god, and Alben has never seen much of a line between the two.

### The Still

Dominating the center of the room: a squat, much-repaired copper still, patched in at least three places with mismatched metal, kept running less through good engineering and more through sheer stubborn familiarity. Alben knows exactly which valve sticks and exactly how hard to hit it. Nobody else could operate this still half as well as he can, and he's never once considered that a problem worth fixing.

### The Ferment Casks

Along one wall, a rack of sealed fermenting casks in various stages, each chalked with a date and a terse note in Alben's blocky handwriting — "too soft," "good fight," "needs more under-ripe." No formal ledger. Just the casks and his memory, which has never once let him down on a batch.

### The Ruining Station

A dedicated workbench holding jars of charred stave shavings and ground rambutan pit — the components of **the Ruining**, his signature step (see [rambui.md](../rambui/rambui.md#ingredients--preparation)). This is the one station in the room he never lets anyone else touch, staff or otherwise, even after the Sanctum stops being the only distillery he owns.

### The Shrine Nook

Tucked into a corner opposite the still: a small shrine to **Cayden Cailean**, little more than a shelf, a tankard-shaped icon, and a scorched brandy cask serving as an altar. Alben prays here the same way he brews — informally, out loud, mid-task, more conversation than ceremony. He's been known to bless a batch and toast his god in the same breath, using the same cup.

### Solomon's Perch

A modest wooden perch beside the shrine, worn smooth from use. This is where Solomon sits during the QA Tester step (see [rambui.md](../rambui/rambui.md#ingredients--preparation)) — close enough to the shrine that Alben has, more than once, joked that the duck is as much a fixture of his faith as Cayden Cailean's tankard is.

### The Duck Run

Behind the shrine nook, low in the wall where the stone gives way to the old cask chute, there is a hole.

It is **ten inches wide and fourteen inches tall**, framed in scrap plank, and it goes up. It follows the line of the disused chute through the foundation, turns twice, and comes out under the bottom step of the alley stair behind a board that looks nailed down and isn't. Inside, Alben laid a plank ramp with cleats every few inches — Solomon walks, he doesn't climb, and he was never going to manage a ladder no matter how patiently anyone explained it to him.

Alben cut it the same week he finished the still, and he has never once explained why to anyone who asked.

The reason is simple enough. The Sanctum is a stone box with exactly one way in and out, and that way is a narrow stair. Alben worked that out standing at the bottom of it one night with a cup in his hand, and what he thought about was not thieves or fire. He thought about a man dying on a bar with no one in the room who knew what to do, and a duck standing on his chest with nowhere to go (see [background.md](../background.md#how-they-met)). Then he put the cup down, found a chisel, and started cutting.

**The Rule.** Everything Alben has ever built for Solomon since obeys three conditions he laid down here, at the first hole in the first wall, without ever writing them anywhere:

1. **Every door on the run opens from the inside, with a bill, without hands.** No latch Solomon can't work himself.
2. **Never fewer than two ways out of any room Solomon sits in.** The Sanctum had one. Now it has two.
3. **Nothing on the run may ever stop Solomon leaving.** Whatever it keeps out, it keeps out going *in*. This one matters later, when there's magic involved.

**What's at the top.** A folded square of oilcloth, tucked under the loose board, so Solomon doesn't have to stand in alley mud while he waits. Alben replaces it when it rots. He has never mentioned it to anybody.

**The lintel.** Scratched into the plank frame at the bottom of the run is a crooked tankard, about the size of a thumbnail, and Alben blessed it out loud the day he cut it — a full toast, cup raised, in an empty cellar, to a duck who wasn't paying attention. It is not a spell. It does nothing. He knows it does nothing. He re-scratches it whenever the wood wears smooth.

> **GM Note — Mechanics.** The Duck Run admits **Tiny creatures only**; Alben cannot fit, and that is the entire point — this is Solomon's route, not an escape hatch for its owner. Alben's own bail-out procedure is a separate matter entirely (see [escapeplan.md](../plans/escapeplan.md)). There is **no magic on the run at this stage of Alben's life** — no wards, no alarms, no light. Solomon has low-light vision, and the run is dark; treat the middle stretch as **dim to no light** and let that cost him a round if it matters. Finding the alley board from outside is **Perception DC 17** (it reads as foundation trim); the board itself is unlocked, because of Rule 1. A hostile Tiny creature can use the run in either direction, and nothing here prevents it. That vulnerability is deliberate, unresolved, and the reason later versions of the run get wards on them.

## The Ritual of the Room

Nothing about the Sanctum is public, and nothing about it is meant to be. This is where Alben goes to brew, to pray, and — more often than either — to simply sit with Solomon and a fresh batch and not perform being Uncle Alben for anyone. It is the one space, in every iteration of his life since, that has never had a bar, a crowd, or a fighting pit anywhere near it.

---

## From Sanctum to Sippin' Club

The Sanctum was never meant to hold customers, and for a long while, it didn't. Eventually, though, word of Alben's brewing outgrew what a solo traveling cleric could give away one cup at a time, and he built a small front room above it to actually receive the people who kept showing up looking for a drink and a chat. That front room became [The Little Church](littlechurch.md) — and the Sanctum, rather than being abandoned, simply became its basement and back office, doing exactly what it always did, just with a floor of paying regulars over its head instead of open sky.
