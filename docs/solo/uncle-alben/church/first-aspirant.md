# The First Aspirant — Juna, called "First Light"

> The first person, besides Alben and Solomon, to become a permanent fixture of the [Little Church](littlechurch.md). Her near-death in the pit is the actual origin of the exact wording of [the Nightly Promise](littlechurch.md#the-nightly-promise) — "Nobody stays broken past sunup" isn't a turn of phrase, it's a fact about one specific morning. She is also, today, the "Ruining-adjacent apprentice" referenced in the [Big Church's Staff Quarters](bigchurch.md#4th-floor--training-and-operations).

---

## Who She Is
 
A half-orc, somewhere in her late twenties now, built like someone who's spent more time being thrown than throwing — wiry rather than heavy, all forearms and old scar tissue, with a gap-toothed grin she deploys constantly and a laugh entirely too loud for most rooms. She showed up at the Little Church in its first few months, back when the building was still mostly just Alben, Solomon, a bar, and a hole in the floor with a rope around it that generously called itself a pit.

She'd been drifting the pit-fighting circuit for a couple years by then — small-town wagering rings, dockside brawls, anywhere a stranger with fast hands and no reputation to protect could pick up a purse. She'd heard there was a new place with a real pit and a barkeep who healed you after, and figured that sounded like the easiest money she'd find all season. She was wrong about the "easy" part almost immediately.

---

## The Night She Almost Died

The Little Church didn't have a reputation yet, which meant it didn't have a *matchmaking* system yet either — Alben just let whoever wanted to fight, fight. On Juna's first night, that meant she got thrown in against a visiting brawler twice her size passing through with a caravan, a man with nothing to lose and no interest in a friendly bout. It was not a friendly bout.

She went down hard, three rounds in, in a way that made the whole room go quiet before anyone even really registered what they were looking at. Alben — who by this point had stabilized plenty of bar fights with a hands-on Medicine check and a stern word — realized within seconds that a Medicine check wasn't going to be enough. He vaulted the rail, got a hand on her chest, and cast *Stabilize* before she'd even finished collapsing. It held. Barely.

What came after wasn't yet "the Promise" — it wasn't a policy, it was just Alben, kneeling in a half-finished pit at two in the morning, refusing to let a stranger die in his half-finished bar over a fight he shouldn't have allowed in the first place. He spent every Font slot he had. He sat with her the rest of the night when the healing alone wasn't quite enough to know she'd wake up right, Solomon perched on her sternum the whole time the way he does, watching her breathe. She opened her eyes as the sun actually came up through the Little Church's one east-facing window — fully healed, fully alive, and, as she tells it, "furious that I'd missed the whole fight I was supposedly in."

Alben has told the story exactly the same way every time since, usually right before he says the line: *"Nobody dies in my house. Nobody stays broken past sunup."* He didn't have that line before that night. He's had it every night since.

---

## Why She Stayed

By all reasonable accounts, Juna should have taken her lumps, cursed her luck, and moved on to the next ring in the next town the way she always had. Instead, she stuck around to watch Alben close up that morning, then showed up again that evening uninvited, and kept showing up every night after that until nobody — including Alben — quite remembers the exact day she stopped being a stranger who wouldn't leave and started being staff.

Her own explanation, when she bothers giving one, is characteristically blunt: *"Nobody's ever put me back together like that before. Wasn't about to just walk off and let that be a one-time thing."* She wasn't interested in becoming a cleric, and Alben never pushed it — but she wanted in on whatever this place was building, badly enough to ask Alben to teach her, which is the part that actually surprised him. Nobody had ever asked Uncle Alben to *teach* them anything before. He said yes before he'd thought it through, which is more or less how he says yes to everything.

She started as the person who swept up after fights and hauled empty casks. She is, today, the reason the Little Church could ever have become anything bigger than one room.

---

## Her Role Today

Juna is the **first Aspirant** — a title that didn't exist before her and now quietly describes anyone who comes to the Church wanting to learn the whole operation rather than just drink in it, fight in it, or work a single shift of it. She was the only one for years. She is not the only one anymore, but she is the one every later Aspirant answers to.

- **Ruining-Adjacent Apprentice.** She is the staff member referenced at the [Big Church's 4th Floor Staff Quarters](bigchurch.md#4th-floor--training-and-operations) — "the closest thing Alben has to a successor-in-training for parts of the process he can't personally handle at this scale." Alben still never lets anyone touch the actual Ruining step itself (see [rambui.md](../rambui/rambui.md#ingredients--preparation)), Juna included — but everything adjacent to it is hers: managing the Ferment Casks' schedules, running the Production Casking Hall, keeping the Ruining Vault properly stocked and secured, and knowing exactly when a batch is ready to be walked in to Alben personally.
- **Operations, in practice.** She effectively runs what the Big Church's floor plan calls the Operations Offices — scheduling fighters into the pit, managing the Store's stock, keeping the Training Hall running — freeing Alben to spend his nights on the floor instead of behind a desk, exactly the arrangement that entry describes.
- **Still fights, occasionally.** She hasn't left the pit behind entirely. She referees more than she competes these days, but she'll still step in for an Octagon bout on a slow night, usually against newer Aspirants as an informal test — nobody graduates out of "just staff" and into "trusted with the Vault" without going a few rounds with Juna first, win or lose.
- **Trains the ones who come after.** Every Aspirant since has gone through some version of what she went through — not the near-death part on purpose, obviously, but the long slow process of showing up, being useful, and being taught. She's harder on new Aspirants than Alben ever is, on the theory that she's the one who has to trust them with the Vault eventually.

---

## Faith

Juna isn't a cleric and has never pretended otherwise, but she's picked up more of Cayden Cailean's creed by osmosis than she'd probably admit — she pours a drink for anyone who looks like they need one before they ask, she's never once turned away someone who couldn't pay for a bunk, and she genuinely can't stand watching anyone get pushed around, in or out of the pit. Alben has offered, more than once and always half-joking, to put in a good word for her with his god. She always tells him her cut of the Store's profits is thanks enough.

---

## Voice & Presence

Loud in a different register than Alben — sharper, faster, more likely to be settling a dispute between two drunk regulars than telling a story to a full room. Where Alben is warmth first and force second, Juna runs the opposite way: she leads with a raised eyebrow and a flat *"you sure about that?"* and lets the warmth show up after, usually in the form of buying the loser's next round herself.

> *"First rule of this place, the one Alben never actually says out loud because he doesn't think he needs to: you don't gotta win. You just gotta still be here in the morning. I'm proof that's the only rule that ever mattered."*

## Optional NPC Sketch (GM Use)

Not a full character sheet — Juna is written as a supporting NPC, not a build meant for play. If she's ever needed on the table (a sparring partner, a source of local information, a complication if the Vault or Operations Office is threatened), a **level 4–6 Fighter or Gladiator-archetype human/half-orc brawler** with Expert Athletics, trained Medicine (picked up over years of watching Alben work), and no spellcasting of her own is an appropriate baseline — scale her level to whatever the table's current party level calls for, since her role in the story matters far more than her stat block ever will.
